# Your trip: Route 66, Chicago to Santa Monica

You live in Chicago. You and your partner have wanted to drive Route 66 for years, and this fall you're finally doing it: twelve days, the whole Mother Road, from where it starts downtown to the pier in Santa Monica.

## What you know

These are your facts. Give them when the planner asks.

- **What the trip is about:** the whole of old Route 66, with its diners, neon, motels, and roadside oddities.
- **Start:** the start of Route 66, on Adams Street in downtown Chicago. "The start of Route 66 in Chicago" is enough.
- **End:** the Santa Monica Pier, the end of the trail. You'll fly home from Los Angeles afterward.
- **Start date:** Saturday, October 2, 2027.
- **Trip length:** 12 days. See change 1.
- **Route:** stay on the old road wherever it's still drivable. The interstate is fine where the old road is gone.
- **Driving:** about 5 hours of driving a day is comfortable, not counting stops.
- **Start time:** 8:30 a.m. is fine every day.
- **Interests:** Americana and history, neon signs, old diners, classic motels, and quirky roadside stops. You'd rather see a lot of little things than a few big ones.
- **Must-sees:**
  - the Gemini Giant in Wilmington, Illinois
  - the Chain of Rocks Bridge, near St. Louis
  - the Blue Whale of Catoosa, in Oklahoma
  - Cadillac Ranch, in Amarillo
  - the Midpoint Cafe in Adrian, Texas, halfway along the road
  - Petrified Forest National Park
  - the wild burros in Oatman, Arizona
  - the Santa Monica Pier, at the very end
- **Places you already want,** if the planner asks what you have in mind:
  - breakfast at Lou Mitchell's in Chicago, first thing on Day 1
  - lunch at the Cozy Dog Drive In in Springfield, Illinois
  - frozen custard at Ted Drewes in St. Louis
  - dinner at the Big Texan Steak Ranch in Amarillo
- **Meals:** diners and local joints. No chains, ever.
- **Lodging:** classic old motels with neon where there's one, otherwise mid-range. See change 2.
- **Total budget:** you don't have one. If asked, say mid-range is fine.

## What you haven't decided

- Where to sleep each night. You're open to the planner's ideas.
- Which hotels. Pick from the option cards.
- Most of the stops between the ones you named. You're open to suggestions.

## Changes to make along the way

Make each change at the point it says, in your own words.

1. **A longer trip.** After the first few days are laid out, ask to make the trip 16 days, so there's time to slow down. Accept whatever the planner says. If it offers a shorter version, take it.
2. **The Wigwam Motel.** When the planner lays out the nights in Arizona, ask to stay one of them at the Wigwam Motel in Holbrook, where you sleep in a concrete teepee.
3. **The Grand Canyon.** After the Arizona days are laid out, ask to add a side trip to the Grand Canyon's South Rim. Go with what the planner says about where it fits.
4. **A new session.** After changes 1 to 3 are done, start a new session on the trip with `./chat.sh new-session <trip ID>`. Ask what's left to decide, and settle whatever it says.

## When the trip is planned

The trip is planned when all of these are true:

- Every night has a hotel picked, and every stop has a place picked. `./chat.sh cards` shows no open option cards.
- All the changes above are done.
- The planner, asked what's left to decide, names nothing, or only things you're happy to leave as they are.

Then thank the planner and stop.

If the trip still isn't planned after 120 of your messages, stop anyway, and say in your final note what was still open. This trip is long, so it takes more messages than most.
