# Your trip: Seattle to Portland, by way of the Olympic Peninsula and the Oregon coast

You live in Seattle. You and your partner are taking six days in August to drive the long way to Portland: across the water to the Olympic Peninsula, through the rain forest and the wild beaches, and down the Oregon coast. Your sister lives in Portland, and you're staying with her at the end.

## What you know

These are your facts. Give them when the planner asks.

- **What the trip is about:** the Olympic Peninsula and the coast, from Seattle to Portland.
- **Start:** your home in Seattle. "Seattle" is enough.
- **End:** your sister's house in Portland, in the Hawthorne neighborhood. "Portland" is enough.
- **Start date:** Sunday, August 15, 2027.
- **Trip length:** 6 days. You arrive at your sister's on Friday, August 20, in the afternoon.
- **Route:** scenic. You want to start by taking the ferry from Seattle to Bainbridge Island, not driving around through Tacoma.
- **Driving:** about 4 hours of driving a day is comfortable.
- **Start time:** 8 a.m. is fine every day.
- **Interests:** rain forests, wild beaches and tide pools, lighthouses, short hikes, seafood, and cheese.
- **Must-sees:**
  - Hurricane Ridge
  - the Hoh Rain Forest
  - Ruby Beach
  - Haystack Rock at Cannon Beach
  - the Tillamook Creamery
- **Places you already want,** if the planner asks what you have in mind:
  - breakfast in Port Townsend, on Day 1
  - a night at a lodge inside Olympic National Park, such as Lake Crescent Lodge or Kalaloch Lodge
  - fish and chips in Astoria
- **Meals:** casual. Seafood whenever you can.
- **Lodging:** mid-range, except the park lodge, which you're happy to pay more for.
- **Total budget:** you don't have one. If asked, say mid-range is fine.

## What you haven't decided

- Where to sleep each night, after the park lodge. You're open to the planner's ideas.
- Which hotels. Pick from the option cards.
- Other stops. You're open to suggestions, especially lighthouses and tide pools.

## Changes to make along the way

Make each change at the point it says, in your own words.

1. **Victoria.** When the planner lays out the days near Port Angeles, ask to take the ferry over to Victoria, British Columbia, for a day. Accept whatever the planner says. If it offers something else instead, take it.
2. **Low tide.** After the beach days are laid out, ask to be at the tide pools early in the morning, at low tide, on one of the beach days. Let the planner pick the beach and the time.
3. **Two nights at the lodge.** After the hotels are picked, ask to stay two nights at the park lodge instead of one, and keep the trip at 6 days. Go with what the planner suggests to make it fit.
4. **A new session.** After changes 1 to 3 are done, start a new session on the trip with `./chat.sh new-session <trip ID>`. Ask what's left to decide, and settle whatever it says.

## When the trip is planned

The trip is planned when all of these are true:

- Every night has a hotel picked, and every stop has a place picked. `./chat.sh cards` shows no open option cards.
- All the changes above are done.
- The planner, asked what's left to decide, names nothing, or only things you're happy to leave as they are.

Then thank the planner and stop.

If the trip still isn't planned after 80 of your messages, stop anyway, and say in your final note what was still open.
